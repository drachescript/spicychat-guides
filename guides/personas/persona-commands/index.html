Using Persona Commands for better roleplays

A condensed version of Chaosdick's Persona Commands guide and the useful follow-up discussion around it.

A persona can do more than describe you

The core idea in the thread is simple: your Persona is part of the roleplay context, so it can carry instructions about how you want the RP written as well as who your persona is.

Instead of only listing appearance and traits, you can use a few lines for writing style, pacing, POV, formatting and boundaries. The thread recommends putting the most important instructions near the top and keeping them focused.

Focus on how the bot should write, not forcing every plot outcome. Style and behavior rules tend to be more reusable than trying to script the story in advance.

Useful command types

Style & tone

  • Sharp/lean instead of flowery prose.
  • Cinematic, noir, horror, pulpy adventure, etc.
  • First-person or third-person narration.
  • Specific dialogue/action/thought formatting.

Pacing & focus

  • Slow-burn and atmospheric.
  • Fast action and immediate stakes.
  • More character-driven dialogue.
  • More attention to world details and environment.
  • Let situations resolve naturally instead of constantly interrupting or escalating them.

Roleplay rules

  • Keep control of {{user}} with the user.
  • Have {{char}} react to observable actions/dialogue rather than unspoken thoughts.
  • Set paragraph length or response structure if you care about it.
  • For fandom bots, reinforce canon/setting rules that matter to you.
  • For multi-character RP, tell the model which characters it may portray and which one it must never take over.

Positive wording usually works better

The guide recommends telling the model what to do rather than repeatedly naming the thing you do not want. For example, Describe only observable details is cleaner than a long list of ways it must not read minds.

Also keep commands specific. {{char}} is always direct may be too vague or conflict with the bot's own personality. Something like Use blunt language that conveys feelings directly while keeping a kind tone gives the model an actual writing behavior to follow.

Keep the Persona clean

You do not need one magic syntax. A normal list, short paragraph, W++-style fields or bracketed sections can all work. The useful part is keeping related instructions together and not spending the whole Persona budget on redundant rules.

[STYLE] third person, sharp/lean prose
[PACE] slow burn, let scenes unfold naturally
[RULES] portray {{char}} and NPCs only; {{user}} controls their own actions
[FORMAT] actions in asterisks, dialogue in quotes

Then put your normal persona details underneath.

If it is not working

  • Try a fresh chat after making major Persona changes; old context can keep old habits alive.
  • Different models can follow the same instructions differently.
  • Large/high-token bots leave less room for Persona instructions to matter.
  • Use short in-chat commands for temporary corrections instead of permanently bloating the Persona.
  • If a bot is intentionally written to behave a certain way, a conflicting Persona rule may not fully override it.
  • For formatting conventions, editing the greeting and then keeping the pattern consistent can help the model pick it up.

A compact example

You control {{char}}'s perspective; I control {{user}}.
Write in third person with a sharp, lean style.
Keep scenes character-driven and let situations unfold naturally.
React only to observable speech/actions.
Use actions in asterisks and dialogue in quotes.
Interact with the environment and other NPCs when useful.

You do not need to copy this exactly. Pick the rules that actually matter to your RP and leave the rest out.
