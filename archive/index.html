SpicyChat Archive — Full Guide

old records, versions, deleted listings, recovery and Archive search.

what is the SpicyChat Archive?

lost a bot and trying to recover it? start with the Archive for Dummies recovery walkthrough. it shows how to find the bot ID, use saved chat evidence and make a new character step by step.

it's a separate, independent site that saves information from publicly available SpicyChat bots, Lorebooks and Rulebook listings over time. the point is to have something to look back at when a creator changes a bot, a listing disappears or you can't remember what an older version said.

the site is spicychatarchive.drache.uk. it's not an official SpicyChat product, and it isn't a collection of everybody's private conversations. what it can show depends on what was public and what the Archive actually managed to collect.

this guide is based on the Archive repository, its public pages and the README checked on November 19, 2026.

1. where do i start?

the homepage has links to Deleted bots, Deleted Lorebooks, Deleted Rulebooks and Recovery Lab.

if you already know the name or ID of a bot, i'd start with the Explorer or Archive Search instead of scrolling through the deleted list.

2. seen, saved, public and confirmed gone aren't the same

this part is pretty important, because the homepage has several different counts and they're not supposed to add up to one magic total.

StatusWhat it means
Seenthe Archive recorded a bot ID at some point, but might not have a complete saved file
Savedthere's a bot record, although some fields might still be missing
PublicSpicyChat currently reports the bot as publicly listed
Confirmed gonethe Archive saw repeated explicit unavailability evidence, not just one failed search
Unknown / inconclusivethere wasn't enough reliable evidence to decide

so if the Archive says it has seen far more bot IDs than it has full saved files, that isn't automatically a problem. and a bot disappearing from SpicyChat search isn't proof it was deleted. the FAQ explains the distinction in more detail.

3. finding a bot by name or ID

try the Explorer when you want to browse records, and Archive Search when you want to look through saved historical text.

an exact bot ID is the safest way to avoid mixing up characters with similar names. if you still have a SpicyChat bot URL, keep it—those IDs are often much more useful for recovery than a title you only half remember.

not finding something doesn't prove it never existed. maybe the Archive hadn't discovered that public bot yet, the file wasn't completely saved, or it was removed before the next scan.

4. older versions and changes

if the Archive saved a bot before and after a creator edit, you can inspect the recorded versions and changes. this is useful when a Greeting, title, scenario, tags or other public metadata changed and you want to see what was there before.

the Archive tries to distinguish actual content edits from noisy activity updates. a higher message count, recommendation score or automatically changed timestamp isn't automatically a new creator revision.

for recent changes, there's a Changes page and Activity page. not every recorded bot will have a complete previous definition, so treat a missing older field as missing evidence rather than a confirmed empty field.

5. what's on the Deleted pages?

Deleted bots focuses on characters the Archive has directly checked and confirmed unavailable. Deleted Lorebooks and Deleted Rulebooks track their own types of disappearance.

Rulebook archival is particularly easy to misunderstand: preserving an old public listing doesn't necessarily mean the Archive has every meter, trigger or private rule that existed behind it. don't assume a missing public Rulebook listing proves the creator deleted the underlying definition.

6. how does it decide a bot is gone?

the Archive doesn't label something deleted just because it vanishes from search or one request times out. its FAQ describes repeated direct checks against SpicyChat's public character endpoint, including some cases where an empty response is treated as unavailable by the website.

temporary errors, authorization failures and incomplete responses aren't supposed to count as deletion evidence. that's why there are suspect, inconclusive and recheck states instead of simply marking every missing result as gone.

if an old bot becomes public again, a later verification can identify that change. the Archive is a historical record, not a permanent verdict on what SpicyChat will show tomorrow.

7. Lorebooks and their history

there are dedicated Lorebooks and Lorebook detail pages. saved public details and older entries can be useful for seeing how a world or character changed.

a public Lorebook and one attached privately to a bot are not the same thing. if a creator never published certain entries, the Archive can't promise to recover them.

for actually building your own, see our Lorebook guide. the Archive is for looking up saved history, not a replacement for the live SpicyChat editor.

8. Rulebooks in the Archive

the Archive now has Rulebook listings, detail pages and a no-longer-public listing.

some creators publish their rules and others keep them hidden. the Archive can only preserve public information it was actually able to observe. a list of meter names doesn't mean the complete rules were saved.

for creating a Rulebook rather than looking at its history, use Rulebook Basics or Advanced Rulebook Design.

9. Recovery Lab — when a bot is missing

Recovery Lab helps collect whatever evidence still exists for a missing character. it can work with archived records and information you provide, such as a saved conversation export.

the goal is a reviewable reconstruction, not to pretend a guessed Personality was the exact original. some details can be recovered from real saved evidence; others may need to be written again and should be labeled accordingly.

  1. start with the bot's exact URL or ID, if you still have it.
  2. check whether the Archive has a record or older version.
  3. gather your own export or screenshots if the original information is incomplete.
  4. review the evidence and any reconstructed draft field by field.
  5. keep a copy before moving any text into a new private character.

if you have a full creator-owned backup, using that is usually better than reconstructing from incomplete evidence.

10. Merge Lab and combining evidence

the Merge Lab is another tool for working with partial archive/recovery records. it can help compare and bring together information from different evidence sources.

don't interpret a merged field as authenticated original content unless you know where it came from. if sources contradict each other, keep the older/newer dates and which record supplied the text.

11. stats, trends and the less obvious tools

the Archive also has Stats, Names & Trends and Tag Stats. these are handy if you're curious about creators, popular names and older tags.

historical tags can stay searchable after SpicyChat removes them from its current picker. that doesn't mean they're valid options for new creations today.

there are also specialized pages for Inconclusive records and flagged material. those are about reviewing particular records, not a replacement for the normal search experience.

12. Community Themes and Blocking Presets

the Archive provides optional public data that DragonScript QoL can use for Community Themes and Archive Blocking Presets. you can preview the latter at Blocking Presets.

those presets are for personal filtering preferences. they're all off by default, and they don't automatically block or dislike bots on SpicyChat. matching a character or franchise from a short title can be uncertain, especially with original characters or group titles.

Community Themes are separate from official SpicyChat tags. some labels are reviewed while others are only candidates. unknown means there isn't matching evidence, not that a bot has been proven free of the theme.

the Archive↔QoL integration notes explain the read-only public data feeds and why neither project needs simultaneous releases.

13. what the Archive doesn't save

the Archive is based on publicly observable data, not your account's private chat history, personal Personas, hidden Rulebook definitions or authenticated drafts. an archive of public information isn't a way around a creator's private visibility settings.

Recovery Lab can use evidence you choose to provide. think about what you're sharing before uploading private conversation exports or personal information anywhere, and don't republish another person's private character as if you had permission.

14. when a result looks wrong

check the bot ID, the date of the archived version and whether the status says saved, unknown, public or confirmed gone. many confusing results come from comparing a historical record with a current listing.

if a record is incomplete, don't assume the missing field was blank. if a disappearance seems wrong, remember that a temporary SpicyChat failure can look like deletion until another check confirms it.

the Archive FAQ has more about the scanning and verification process. for implementation details or a reproducible problem, there's the Archive GitHub Issues page.
