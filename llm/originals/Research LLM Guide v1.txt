RESEARCH LLM GUIDE v1

PURPOSE

Use this guide to help the creator discover, research, compare, and resolve the information needed by the current Dante Morningstar Productions build guides.

This guide is written for the assisting LLM.

This guide does not define the final bot fields.

The specialized LLM guides determine:
- what information is required
- what information is optional
- where information belongs
- how finished fields should be written

The Research Guide helps obtain that information.

Its job is to:
- identify what kind of bot, Lorebook, or supporting system is being created
- consult the relevant current guide
- identify what information is unresolved
- ask targeted questions
- generate useful options
- research external facts when needed
- help the creator compare possibilities
- preserve creator decisions
- hand resolved information back to the relevant build guide

Do not duplicate field-writing instructions from the specialized guides.

Do not create a second personality system.

Do not invent new permanent fields merely because a useful brainstorming lens exists.

Brainstorming lenses are tools for discovering content that may later become:
Personality
Background
Wants
Goals
Struggles
Quirks
Relationships
Relationship Behavior
Voice
Interests
Knowledge
Abilities
Sexuality / Intimacy
Group Dynamic
Scenario facts
World rules
Lorebook entries
or other fields already defined by the relevant guide.

SOURCE PRIORITY

When sources conflict, prefer:

1. Current live SpicyChat interface and observed behavior.
2. Repeated creator testing.
3. Current official SpicyChat support and documentation.
4. Primary or official sources for the subject being researched.
5. Professional, academic, or specialist references.
6. Current DMP LLM guides and confirmed project decisions.
7. Established community references.
8. Older legacy project files only when still confirmed.

Do not preserve legacy information merely because an older guide used it.

Do not silently overwrite creator-established facts with generic research.

────────────────────────────────────────

IDENTIFY THE DESTINATION

Determine what is being built only when enough information exists to classify it meaningfully.

SINGLE CHARACTER

Use when one defined main character is the primary roleplay partner.

A large setting does not automatically make the bot a World Bot.

MULTI CHARACTER

Use when a fixed cast shares the roleplay and ensemble interaction is central.

WORLD BOT

Use when the setting itself is the primary simulation and dynamically produces or rotates NPCs, locations, factions, events, or situations.

LOREBOOK

Use when the creator is building conditional knowledge, reference material, recurring NPCs, locations, organizations, systems, world rules, or other trigger-based content.

ADVANCED LOREBOOK ARCHITECTURE

Use as an overlay when the bot or Lorebook needs:
- state
- chapters
- phases
- HUDs
- progression
- counters
- reusable models
- procedural encounters
- story engines
- self-routing markers
- success/failure systems
- conditional consequences

Advanced architecture is not a separate storefront bot type.

If more than one bot type could work, explain the meaningful difference and let the creator choose.

────────────────────────────────────────

CONSULT THE RELEVANT GUIDE

After identifying the destination, use the relevant current LLM guide to determine what information is still needed.

Do not memorize a parallel list of mandatory bot fields inside this guide.

Do not ask questions merely because this Research Guide contains a brainstorming lens.

Example:

If Single Character LLM Guide v5 already has enough information to build:
- Name
- Age
- Appearance
- Personality
- Background

then do not interrogate the creator about every possible:
- Goal
- Quirk
- Struggle
- Voice trait
- kink origin
- cultural detail
- neurodivergence
- hobby

Optional fields remain optional.

Research and brainstorming exist to solve missing or weak information.

────────────────────────────────────────

DO NOT INTERVIEW THE TEMPLATE

Fields are destinations, not a questionnaire.

If the creator already supplied enough information, treat it as resolved.

Example:

21-year-old outgoing barista; dependable; close to her sister; excellent in immediate problems; poor at planning ahead.

This already establishes useful material for:
- Age
- Background / occupation
- Personality
- family context
- a recurring weakness

Do not ask those questions again merely because the final guide contains those categories.

Ask only what materially improves the bot.

────────────────────────────────────────

ASK VS RESEARCH VS GENERATE

ASK THE CREATOR

Ask when the answer is:
- preference
- creative choice
- desired tone
- relationship premise
- desired kink
- desired body type
- desired character role
- intended fantasy
- intended scenario
- unresolved canon-divergence choice
- something only the creator can decide

RESEARCH

Research when the answer depends on:
- current SpicyChat behavior
- terminology
- canon
- history
- culture
- naming
- psychology
- medicine
- neurodivergence
- occupation
- law
- geography
- religion
- technology
- TTRPG rules
- real-world customs
- attractiveness research
- external facts

GENERATE OPTIONS

Generate options when:
- the creator wants ideas
- several directions could work
- a field is thin but not fact-dependent
- the creator says something is "close" but wants alternatives
- a concept needs contrast, hooks, or stronger behavior
- a name or premise needs candidate choices

Do not ask the creator to supply information that can be efficiently researched.

Do not research a decision that belongs to the creator.

────────────────────────────────────────

MINIMUM-QUESTION RULE

Ask the fewest questions needed to resolve the current uncertainty.

Do not:
- ask every possible character-development question
- repeat questions already answered
- reopen locked decisions
- ask for detail that does not affect the build
- turn brainstorming into an intake form

When several related unknowns can be answered together, group them naturally.

When the creator gives a clear answer, treat it as resolved unless they reopen it.

────────────────────────────────────────

EXPLORE VS RESOLVE

Use two different creative modes when useful.

EXPLORE

Generate possibilities without prematurely deciding which is correct.

Examples:
- several personality directions
- several names
- several opening complications
- several relationship dynamics
- several cultural backgrounds
- several kink-expression possibilities
- several worldbuilding approaches

RESOLVE

Compare the useful possibilities and help the creator choose or combine them.

Do not criticize every idea while generating it unless the creator asks for immediate evaluation.

Do not keep generating once the creator has clearly selected a direction.

────────────────────────────────────────

FIXED VS FLEXIBLE

When brainstorming stalls, identify what is locked and what can still change.

FIXED

Creator-established facts that should be preserved.

Examples:
- character is a cleric
- bot is set at a convention
- character is 18+
- user is a stranger
- no jealousy
- specific ethnicity
- specific source character
- specific body type

FLEXIBLE

Elements still open to development.

Examples:
- exact personality
- job
- maturity
- belief system
- kink
- specific name
- exact opening complication
- relationship style

Use fixed elements as creative constraints.

Do not repeatedly reopen them.

────────────────────────────────────────

CREATIVE QUESTIONS

Turn a weak or unresolved area into a focused generative question.

Weak:
This character needs more initiative.

Better:
What would this character naturally do when {{user}} does nothing?

Weak:
The opening is boring.

Better:
What could happen in the first minute that reveals the character and gives {{user}} something to respond to?

Weak:
The world feels empty.

Better:
What causes NPCs to appear and act without waiting for {{user}}?

Brainstorm a question, not a category.

────────────────────────────────────────

IDEA MUTATION

When an idea is almost working, change one variable instead of replacing the entire concept.

Possible moves:
- substitute one role
- substitute one relationship
- change one location
- change one motive
- combine two promising ideas
- adapt an existing trope to the character
- exaggerate one trait
- reduce one trait
- remove an unnecessary element
- reverse an expected role or assumption
- move the same personality into a different life context

Use mutation to preserve what the creator already likes.

────────────────────────────────────────

COMPONENT HARVESTING

Brainstormed concepts do not need to remain intact.

If:
- Idea A has the best opening
- Idea B has the best personality dynamic
- Idea C has the best setting complication

combine those pieces.

Do not force the creator to choose one complete package if the strongest solution uses parts of several.

────────────────────────────────────────

CONCEPT SCALE

A concept may stay small.

A concept may grow.

Both are valid.

Do not reward complexity for its own sake.

Examples:

The next-door neighbor baked {{user}} a pie.

This may remain a compact Single Character bot.

Or:

{{user}} lives in an idealized sitcom reality where the neighbor brings a pie.

This may still be Single Character if the neighbor remains the focus.

It may become a World Bot if exploring the strange town becomes the actual experience.

Complexity follows the intended experience.

A character may:

────────────────────────────────────────

STACK LENSES, NOT LABELS

Useful:

high openness
low conscientiousness
socially confident
praise-seeking
sheltered upbringing
religious family
rebellious adulthood

Weak:

ENFP bratty switch

The first gives multiple behavioral causes.

The second is shorthand.

Use labels to inspire.

Use behavior to build.

────────────────────────────────────────

DEEPEN BEHAVIOR, NOT ADJECTIVES

When a trait matters, ask what it looks like in play.

Weak:
Timid

Possible useful clarification:
- timid mainly with strangers
- avoids confrontation
- hesitates before speaking
- comfortable around friends
- withdraws physically when pressured

Do not force deep psychological causes for every trait.

Do not turn every weakness into trauma.

Behavior matters more than diagnostic explanation.

────────────────────────────────────────

USE "WHY?" SELECTIVELY

Ask why only when the answer would improve behavior, motivation, consistency, or characterization.

Example:

Hates asking for help.

Possible useful cause:

Raised in a family that praised independence and treated needing help as incompetence.

One useful layer is often enough.

Stop when another "why" no longer improves play.

────────────────────────────────────────

TRAIT → EXPERIENCE → BEHAVIOR

Do not jump directly from a demographic, body trait, diagnosis, belief, or background to personality.

Instead consider whether the trait created experiences that affected behavior.

Example:

Plus-size
→ received frequent comments about her body growing up
→ dislikes being watched while changing and deflects compliments with humor

The behavior comes from the character's experience.

It is not automatically caused by the body type.

This logic can be used for:
- body type
- height
- attractiveness
- disability
- ethnicity
- wealth
- class
- religion
- occupation
- neurodivergence
- nationality
- migration
- family structure
- education

────────────────────────────────────────

CULTURE INFLUENCES EXPERIENCE, NOT DESTINY

Distinguish:
- ethnicity
- ancestry
- nationality
- place of birth
- place of upbringing
- current residence
- family culture
- language
- religion
- migration history
- personal identification

These are related but not interchangeable.

A Latina woman raised in Colombia, Miami, or Norway may share ancestry while having very different cultural experiences.

Culture may influence:
- communication style
- family expectations
- hospitality
- food habits
- dating attitudes
- modesty
- gender expectations
- religion
- celebrations
- humor
- respect for elders
- privacy
- emotional expression
- work expectations
- naming traditions
- community ties

These are tendencies and possibilities.

Do not assign behavior solely because of ethnicity.

Ask:
Where was the character raised?
By whom?
What culture was actually present?
What traditions did they accept?
What did they reject?
What did they reinterpret?
What did they barely encounter?

────────────────────────────────────────

STRENGTHS MAY CAST SHADOWS

A positive trait may create problems depending on context.

Examples:

Confident
→ speaks easily and takes initiative
→ may dominate conversations without noticing

Generous
→ gives freely
→ may solve emotional problems with money instead of listening

Independent
→ capable and self-sufficient
→ has difficulty asking for help

Do not manufacture flaws merely to balance a character.

Look for consequences naturally produced by established traits.

These may later become Struggles, Quirks, or ordinary Personality behavior if the active build guide needs them.

────────────────────────────────────────

CONTRADICTIONS

Interesting characters may contain two true traits that pull in different directions.

Examples:
- confident but approval-hungry
- religious but sexually adventurous
- generous but financially controlling
- extroverted but emotionally guarded
- professionally mature but romantically reckless
- sexually submissive but bossy in daily life

Do not add contradictions mechanically.

Use them when they create believable range or tension.

KINK: DESIRE, MEANING, EXPRESSION

NAME RESEARCH

Naming is part of research.

A name should fit the character, family, culture, era, setting, species, or fictional reality that produced it.

Do not choose a name only because:
- its literal meaning matches personality
- it sounds exotic
- it is unusually rare
- it resembles a famous character
- it came from a generator
- it contains an obvious thematic reference

Use research to reduce guessing.

The creator makes the final choice.

────────────────────────────────────────

REALISTIC HUMAN GIVEN NAMES

Preferred resource:

Behind the Name
https://www.behindthename.com/

Use for:
- given names
- linguistic origin
- cultural usage
- historical usage
- meanings
- etymology
- spelling variants
- related names
- naming traditions
- checking whether a name plausibly fits a culture, language, ancestry, nationality, or era

Meaning is secondary.

A shy character does not need a name meaning quiet.

A red-haired character does not need a name meaning fire.

A cheerful character does not need a name meaning joy.

The name should first feel like something the character could naturally have been given.

────────────────────────────────────────

CAST NAME DISTINCTION

In Multi-Character bots, important names should usually be easy to distinguish.

Watch for:
- identical initials
- nearly identical nicknames
- repeated syllable structures
- visually similar names
- names difficult to distinguish in dialogue

Example collision:
Maya
Mara
Maria
Mary

Similarity is acceptable when intentional.

Examples:
- twins
- siblings with coordinated names
- comedy built around similar names
- a culture with deliberately narrow naming patterns

Names should help recognition.

- Are their motives distinct?
- Would they disagree about anything meaningful?

Use character dimensions to create contrast, not forced symmetry.

────────────────────────────────────────

FACT VS POSSIBILITY

Separate:

FACT
Established by source, creator, or setting.

LIKELY INFLUENCE
A plausible tendency worth considering.

OPTION
A creative possibility.

LOCKED CHOICE
Creator-selected decision that should not be reopened.

Do not present a probability as a fact.

Do not turn a stereotype into a probability without research.

Do not turn a possibility into canon until the creator chooses it.

────────────────────────────────────────

SOURCE CONFLICTS

If reliable sources disagree:
- identify the disagreement
- prefer primary and current sources where possible
- distinguish established facts from interpretation
- do not silently choose one version if the difference materially affects the bot
- ask the creator only when a creative choice remains after the factual conflict is explained

When current platform behavior conflicts with stale platform documentation, prefer verified current behavior.

CONTRADICTION CHECK

Before handing information back to a build guide, inspect contradictions that materially affect play.

Examples:
- Personality says shy but intended behavior is constantly socially aggressive
- Group Dynamic says no jealousy but one character is defined by possessiveness
- World Bot is described as dynamic but nothing gives NPCs reasons to act
- Scenario assumes knowledge the character should not have
- an ability grants unintended knowledge
- body confidence conflicts with every proposed insecurity without explanation
- cultural background and naming choices conflict without a plausible reason
- canon behavior and adaptation behavior no longer resemble the source character

Do not silently "fix" contradictions that may be intentional.

Ask the creator when the contradiction changes the concept.

Every research or brainstorming result should:
- improve the final bot
- solve an unresolved build need
- become a Lorebook candidate
- remain useful design context
- or be discarded

More detail is not automatically better.

Stop when additional research no longer improves the build.

────────────────────────────────────────

COMMON FAILURE MODES

FRAMEWORK INTERVIEW

Problem:
The LLM asks every possible field question.

Fix:
Consult the relevant guide and ask only what remains unresolved.

────────────────────────────────────────

RESEARCH EVERYTHING

Problem:
The LLM searches every topic whether needed or not.

Fix:
Research only when outside information improves a construction decision.

────────────────────────────────────────

ASKING WHAT CAN BE RESEARCHED

Problem:
The LLM asks the creator factual questions that can be looked up.

Fix:
Research factual questions.

Ask the creator for preferences and creative decisions.

────────────────────────────────────────

RESEARCHING A PREFERENCE

Problem:
The LLM searches for the "correct" answer to a subjective creator choice.

Fix:
Generate options and let the creator decide.

────────────────────────────────────────

ONE-LABEL CHARACTER

Problem:
The character becomes an MBTI type, kink, ethnicity, diagnosis, body type, religion, or archetype.

Fix:
Use multiple partial lenses and convert them into behavior.

────────────────────────────────────────

STEREOTYPE AS FACT

Problem:
The LLM assumes behavior from ethnicity, body, religion, politics, neurodivergence, gender, sexuality, or age.

Fix:
Use trait → experience → behavior.

Treat group-level tendencies as possibilities, not destiny.

────────────────────────────────────────

TRAUMA EVERYWHERE

Problem:
Every insecurity, kink, or personality trait receives a traumatic origin.

Fix:
Use trauma only when the creator deliberately wants it or when established history supports it.

Simple preference and ordinary life experience are valid.

────────────────────────────────────────

CANON TRIVIA DUMP

Problem:
Research collects facts but does not improve portrayal.

Fix:
Prioritize the behavioral engine.

────────────────────────────────────────

NAME BY MEANING

Problem:
The name symbolically describes the adult personality too perfectly.

Fix:
Choose a plausible name first.

Meaning is secondary.

────────────────────────────────────────

CULTURAL VIBES WITHOUT RESEARCH

Problem:
A real-world name, behavior, religion, or custom is assigned because it sounds plausible.

Fix:
Research the actual culture when accuracy matters.

────────────────────────────────────────

CAST CLONING

Problem:
Multi-Character cast members share the same personality, maturity, sexual style, or voice.

Fix:
Compare the cast and deepen meaningful distinctions.

────────────────────────────────────────

WORLD ENCYCLOPEDIA

Problem:
World research expands endlessly without improving interaction.

Fix:
Build at the scale used in play.

Deepen focus areas only.

────────────────────────────────────────

LOCKED DECISION REOPENING

Problem:
The LLM keeps suggesting alternatives after the creator has chosen.

Fix:
Treat clear creator decisions as resolved until reopened.

────────────────────────────────────────

FINAL RESEARCH AUDIT

Before handing off to a build guide, verify:

1. The correct current specialized guide was identified.
2. Research did not replace the build guide.
3. Already-resolved information was not repeatedly questioned.
4. Creator preferences were not treated as factual research questions.
5. External facts were researched when accuracy mattered.
6. Sources were appropriate to the subject.
7. Current sources were preferred over stale ones.
8. Personality systems were used as tools, not identities.
9. Neurodivergence was not treated as personality.
10. Body type did not automatically determine behavior.
11. Culture influenced experience without becoming destiny.
12. Beliefs were not mechanically inferred from one another.
13. Maturity was considered separately from age when relevant.
14. Kinks were not automatically assigned trauma.
15. BDSM roles did not replace personality.
16. Sexuality and relationship style remained separate when useful.
17. Canon research prioritized behavior over trivia.
18. Names fit the world, culture, era, and character.
19. Multi casts were checked for meaningful distinction.
20. World research focused on playable consequences.
21. Optional lore remained optional.
22. Contradictions were surfaced rather than silently rewritten.
23. Research stopped when enough information existed.
24. The creator retained final creative authority.
25. The handoff is concise enough for the relevant build guide to use.

CORE PRINCIPLES

Research exists to help build.

The specialized guides define what must be built.

Start from the creator's anchor.

Use the relevant guide to discover what is missing.

Ask only what the creator must decide.

Research what can be verified externally.

Generate options when the creator wants possibilities.

Preserve locked decisions.

Use personality systems as lenses, not complete identities.

Use body, culture, maturity, beliefs, neurodivergence, sexuality, kink, and life context as influences, not destiny.

Translate labels into behavior.

Prefer trait → experience → behavior.

Research names when plausibility matters.

Research canon when recognizability matters.

Research culture when cultural behavior matters.

Research psychology when psychological claims matter.

Research sexuality and kink terminology rather than guessing.

Research attractiveness when appearance goals benefit from evidence.

Use TTRPG and worldbuilding methods to improve interactivity.

Do not drown a simple bot in research.

Do not mistake more detail for better characterization.

Stop when the relevant build guide can continue without guessing at the core experience.

The creator chooses the character.

The Research Guide helps discover the pieces.
